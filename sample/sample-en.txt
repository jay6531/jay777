CHAPTER I

The rain came suddenly, the way it always does in this city. Lin pulled his hood lower,
held the borrowed book against his chest, and followed the riverside path home. The
streetlamps dissolved into patches of blurred orange in the puddles, as if someone had
knocked over a lamp.

He was in no hurry. The light in his rented room had been broken for a week, and he had
not bothered to fix it. The room was bare: a desk, a chair, and a pot of ivy on the sill
that he kept forgetting to water.

The rain drummed on the umbrella, dense and steady. He remembered the eaves at his
grandmother's house, water running down the tiles in long strings, striking the stone
slabs and wearing small hollows into them. She would bring out a bowl of hot soup and
say, it's getting cold, finish it before you go.

Back then he always believed the road was long, and time was longer.

CHAPTER II

The secondhand bookshop stood at the far end of the alley. Its wooden sign had faded in
the rain until only two characters remained legible. The bell rang once when he pushed
the door open, and the smell of paper and old wood came out to meet him.

The owner was a quiet old man who did not look up, only pointed toward the back. Lin
nodded and slipped between the shelves.

There was no order here, no categories, only a small hill of books stacked by someone in
a hurry. Among them he found a worn copy of Walden. On the flyleaf, in fountain pen:
May you have a cabin of your own, and a lake of your own.

The handwriting was hasty, the ink already bleeding at the edges. Lin closed the book and
felt something in him shift, lightly, like a door left ajar.
